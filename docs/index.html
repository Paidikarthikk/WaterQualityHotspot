A note on reading the comparison

Every value is a station's annual mean for 2013, after median imputation. A station can fail a check on its mean while meeting it most of the year, or the reverse. Treat flags as a list of places to look at more closely, not as a verdict.

About this project

Water Quality Analysis Across States and Identification of Pollution Hotspots (problem statement PS-65) is an exploratory data analysis and visualization project by Paidi Karthik (roll no. 160125737028), Department of Information Technology, Chaitanya Bharathi Institute of Technology, Hyderabad.

The analysis was done in Python with Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib and Seaborn. This page is generated from the same pipeline: build_site.py reloads the raw CSVs, repeats the notebook's cleaning, and exports the station table the charts read.

How to interpret these numbers

  • Dissolved oxygen: higher is better; below 4 mg/L stresses aquatic life.
  • BOD: oxygen demanded by organic matter; higher means more organic pollution such as sewage.
  • pH: 6.5–8.5 is the acceptable band.
  • Coliforms: indicators of sewage contamination; values span several orders of magnitude, so a few stations dominate averages.
  • Temperature, conductivity, nitrate: no reference value in the dataset, so they are described but not screened.

Data sources and limitations

  • Source: Data.gov.in, Water Quality in India – 2013 (36 CSV files).
  • 1,309 missing cells were filled with column medians; imputed values are estimates and can shift averages.
  • Water-body type is inferred from filenames, not supplied as a column.
  • State names are used as recorded (for example "TAMILNADU" and "TAMIL NADU" appear separately), matching the notebook's grouping.
  • Values are annual means from 2013 and may not reflect present-day conditions.
